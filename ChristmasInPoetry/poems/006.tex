% ---------- Sixth Poem ----------
\poemhead{CAROL}

\begin{verse}
Villagers all, this frosty tide,\\
Let your doors swing open wide,\\
Though wind may follow, and snow beside,\\
Yet draw us in by your fire to bide;\\
\hspace*{2em}Joy shall be yours in the morning!\\[0.7em]

Here we stand in the cold and the sleet,\\
Blowing fingers and stamping feet,\\
Come from far away you to greet--\\
You by the fire and we in the street--\\
\hspace*{2em}Bidding you joy in the morning!\\[0.7em]

For ere one half of the night was gone,\\
Sudden a star has led us on,\\
Raining bliss and benison--\\
Bliss to-morrow and more anon,\\
\hspace*{2em}Joy for every morning!\\[0.7em]

Goodman Joseph toiled through the snow--\\
Saw the star o'er a stable low;\\
Mary she might not further go--\\
Welcome thatch, and litter below!\\
\hspace*{2em}Joy was hers in the morning!\\[0.7em]

And then they heard the angels tell\\
"Who were the first to cry NOWELL?\\
Animals all, as it befell,\\
In the stable where they did dwell!\\
\hspace*{2em}Joy shall be theirs in the morning!"%
\end{verse}

\vspace{0.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textit{Kenneth Grahame}\\
\noindent\textit{From \emph{The Wind in the Willows}; Copyright, 1908, by Charles Scribner's Sons.}\\
